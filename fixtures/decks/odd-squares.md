# Odd numbers build squares

## Original synthetic brief

Prepare a three-minute explanation for an undergraduate research seminar.
Motivate the sum of the first positive odd integers with a square that grows
from side two to side three. State the identity for positive integers,
prove it by induction and use the next square as a worked check. Preserve
the preceding state at each click. Use the same notation in the proof and
in the spoken explanation.

## Speaker script

| Slide | View | Say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Grow a square | 1 | Start with this square of side two. How many cells will a square of side three add? |
| 1 · Grow a square | 2 | The lighter rim has three cells across the top and two down the right. Counting the corner only in the top row gives five new cells. |
| 2 · The odd-sum identity | 1 | For any positive integer n, add the odd terms 2k − 1 for k from 1 to n. The result is n squared. |
| 2 · The odd-sum identity | 2 | At n equals one, the only term is one, and that is one squared. |
| 2 · The odd-sum identity | 3 | Assume the first n terms sum to n squared. The term at k equals n plus one is 2n plus one. Adding it gives the square of n plus one, which completes the induction. |
| 3 · Check the next square | 1 | Going from side three to side four adds four cells across the top and three on the right. The resulting sum is 1 plus 3 plus 5 plus 7, or 16. Each odd term counts a rim. |
